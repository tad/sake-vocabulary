**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月25日**

**漢字**
- 船 — 音読み: セン / 訓読み: ふね、ふな — boat, ship. The long wooden pressing box looks like a boat, so it is called ふね (written 槽 or 船), and the man who runs it is the 船頭（せんどう）.
- 桶 — 音読み: トウ (rarely used) / 訓読み: おけ — tub, vat, bucket. Before steel tanks, every stage of brewing happened in wooden 桶, as in 木桶（きおけ）; you met it already inside 半切り桶（はんぎりおけ）.

**ことば**
- 船頭（せんどう） — the worker in charge of pressing at the 槽（ふね）: filling the 酒袋（さかぶくろ）, stacking them and watching the flow. In everyday Japanese 船頭 means a boatman, as in the proverb 船頭多（おお）くして船（ふね）山（やま）に上（のぼ）る ("too many cooks").
- 追い回し（おいまわし） — the most junior 蔵人（くらびと）, who runs errands and does whatever job is needed: washing, carrying, cleaning. The name comes from being "chased around" all day. Most 杜氏（とうじ） started here.
- 木桶（きおけ） — a large wooden vat (usually cedar) for fermenting or storing sake. Rare after the mid-20th century, now making a comeback; labels often say 木桶仕込み（きおけじこみ） to show the sake was fermented in one.
- 琺瑯タンク（ほうろうタンク） — an enamel-lined steel tank, the standard vessel for 醪（もろみ） and storage in most breweries today. Easy to clean and gives no wood flavor, which is why it replaced the 木桶.

**例文**
昔の蔵（くら）では、若い追い回しが木桶を洗（あら）い、醪（もろみ）が仕上（しあ）がると船頭が槽（ふね）で搾（しぼ）ったが、今は多くの蔵が琺瑯タンクを使っている。
||In the old breweries, the young errand hands washed the wooden vats, and when the mash was ready the pressing man pressed it in the boat-shaped press; today most breweries use enamel-lined tanks.||
