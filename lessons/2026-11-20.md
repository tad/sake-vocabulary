**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月20日**

**漢字**
- 香 — 音読み: コウ、キョウ / 訓読み: か、かお-り、かお-る — fragrance, aroma; to smell sweet. Aroma is one of the main ways sake is judged and described, so this kanji is everywhere on labels and tasting notes: 香（かお）り, aroma, and 吟醸香（ぎんじょうか）, the fruity ginjo aroma. Note the reading か in compounds like 吟醸香 and 移（うつ）り香（が）.

**ことば**
- 吟醸酒（ぎんじょうしゅ） — ginjo sake: made by 吟醸造り with rice polished to 60% or less, and with a distinct aroma and flavor. Brewing alcohol may be added; without it, it is 純米吟醸酒（じゅんまいぎんじょうしゅ）.
- 大吟醸酒（だいぎんじょうしゅ） — daiginjo sake: the ginjo grade with rice polished to 50% or less. The top tier on most breweries' price lists, and the classic style for competitions.
- 純米大吟醸酒（じゅんまいだいぎんじょうしゅ） — junmai daiginjo: rice polished to 50% or less and no added alcohol (just rice, kōji and water). Labels often shorten it to 純米大吟醸.
- 吟醸香（ぎんじょうか） — ginjo aroma: the fruity scent (often compared to apple, pear, melon or banana) that yeast produces during slow, cold fermentation. Its two best-known sources are ethyl caproate (apple-like) and isoamyl acetate (banana-like).

**例文**
米を半分（はんぶん）以下（いか）まで磨（みが）いて低温（ていおん）でゆっくり発酵（はっこう）させた純米大吟醸酒には、果物（くだもの）のような華（はな）やかな吟醸香が生まれる。
||A junmai daiginjo, made from rice polished to less than half its size and fermented slowly at low temperature, develops a showy, fruit-like ginjo aroma.||
