**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月6日**
**漢字**
- 酛 — 音読み: なし / 訓読み: もと — yeast starter. A 国字（こくじ）, a character made in Japan, so it has no on-reading: 酉 (the wine jar) plus 元 (origin). It's the "二" in 一麹、二酛、三造り（いちこうじ、にもと、さんづくり）, and every starter method is named with it, starting with today's 速醸酛（そくじょうもと）.
- 酵 — 音読み: コウ / 訓読み: なし — fermentation, leaven. Almost always paired with 母 in 酵母（こうぼ）, yeast, or with 発 in 発酵（はっこう）, fermentation.

**ことば**
- 酒母（しゅぼ） — the yeast starter: a small, dense batch of kōji, steamed rice and water where a large, healthy yeast population is grown before the main mash. 酒母 is the formal term (you'll see it in brewery texts and on tours); 酛（もと） is the older brewers' word for the same thing.
- 酵母（こうぼ） — yeast. The strain a kura chooses (often one of the numbered 協会酵母（きょうかいこうぼ） from the Brewing Society of Japan) shapes the aroma about as much as any other decision.
- 乳酸（にゅうさん） — lactic acid. The starter has to be acidic so that wild yeasts and bacteria can't take over while the sake yeast multiplies. How that acid gets there is what separates the starter methods.
- 速醸酛（そくじょうもと） — the "quick" starter method: ready-made lactic acid goes in at the very start, instead of waiting for lactic bacteria to make it. Developed around 1910, it takes about two weeks rather than about four, and most sake made today uses it.

**例文**
速醸酛（そくじょうもと）では、まず仕込み水（しこみみず）に乳酸（にゅうさん）を加（くわ）えて雑菌（ざっきん）を抑（おさ）え、その中で酵母（こうぼ）を増（ふ）やして酒母（しゅぼ）を育てる。
||In the quick starter method, lactic acid is first added to the brewing water to keep unwanted microbes in check, and the yeast is multiplied in that environment to raise the yeast starter.||
