**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月12日**

**漢字**
- 槽 — 音読み: ソウ / 訓読み: ふね (brewers' reading; not a 常用 kun-reading) — tank, vat, trough. The moromi is finished, so today we start pressing: 槽 appears in the formal word for pressing, 上槽（じょうそう）, and on its own, read ふね, it is the brewers' press box. Outside sake you know it from 水槽（すいそう）, a fish tank.
- 搾 — 音読み: サク / 訓読み: しぼ-る — to squeeze, to press. 搾（しぼ）る is the everyday verb for pressing sake, and 搾りたて（しぼりたて）, "freshly pressed," is a common label word in winter.

**ことば**
- 上槽（じょうそう） — pressing: separating the finished moromi into clear sake and lees. This is the formal brewing term; brewers also just say 搾り（しぼり）.
- 槽搾り（ふねしぼり） — pressing in a 槽（ふね）, a long box, traditionally wooden, now often steel. Bags of moromi are stacked inside and pressed slowly from above. Gentle but laborious; many breweries now use an accordion-style automatic press instead, 自動圧搾機（じどうあっさくき）.
- 酒袋（さかぶくろ） — the cloth bag filled with moromi for pressing in a 槽 or for hanging. Traditionally cotton, now usually synthetic fabric.
- 袋吊り（ふくろづり） — hanging the filled bags and letting the sake drip out by its own weight, with no pressure at all. Used for competition sake and top 大吟醸（だいぎんじょう）; the drops collected this way are sold as 雫酒（しずくざけ）.

**例文**
この蔵（くら）では今も槽搾りで上槽し、特別な大吟醸（だいぎんじょう）だけは袋吊りで搾（しぼ）る。
||At this brewery they still press in a traditional press box, and only the special daiginjō is left to drip from hanging bags.||
