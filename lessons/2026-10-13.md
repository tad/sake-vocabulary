**漢字**
- 卸 — 音読み: シャ / 訓読み: おろす — to grind down; also to unload or sell wholesale. In the kura it lives in 山卸し, the grinding of the starter mash; on the business side you'll see it in 卸売（おろしうり）, wholesale distribution.
- 廃 — 音読み: ハイ / 訓読み: すたれる — to abolish, discontinue, fall out of use. It gives the 山廃 starter its name ("the grinding, abolished"), and it is the 廃 of 廃業（はいぎょう）, a brewery closing its doors.
**ことば**
- 生酛（きもと） — the traditional starter method: natural lactic acid bacteria from the kura environment acidify the starter over about four weeks, after the rice and kōji have been ground to a paste. Often credited with fuller, more layered, sometimes tangy sake, though the brewer's hand matters as much as the method.
- 山卸し（やまおろし） — the grinding of rice, kōji and water into a paste with wooden poles, done in shifts in the cold. Also called 酛摺り（もとすり）. It's the backbreaking step that defines 生酛.
- 山廃酛（やまはいもと） — the starter method that drops 山卸し: research at the national brewing institute around 1909 showed that letting the kōji's enzymes break the rice down in water does the job without grinding. It still relies on natural lactic bacteria. On labels it's usually shortened to 山廃.
- 半切り（はんぎり） — the wide, shallow wooden tub in which a 生酛 starter is divided and ground. It's shallow so the mash stays cold and can be worked by hand; a row of them is the classic image of 山卸し.
**例文**
生酛では半切りに分けた酛を山卸しで摺（す）りつぶすが、山廃酛ではこの重労働を省き、麹の力に任せる。
||In the traditional kimoto method, the starter is divided into shallow wooden tubs and ground to a paste with poles; the yamahai method skips this heavy labor and leaves the work to the power of the kōji.||
