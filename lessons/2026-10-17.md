**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 10月17日**
**漢字**
- 醸 — 音読み: ジョウ / 訓読み: かも-す — to brew, ferment; the core verb of sake-making. You see it in 醸造（じょうぞう）brewing, 醸造元（じょうぞうもと）the brewer named on a label, and the craft phrase 酒を醸す.
- 留 — 音読み: リュウ、ル / 訓読み: と-める、と-まる — to stop, fasten, hold; in the mash it marks the closing addition, 留添（とめぞえ）, and outside the tank it gives 蒸留（じょうりゅう）distillation, as in the 蒸留酒 that becomes brewer's alcohol.
**ことば**
- 品温（ひんおん） — the temperature of the mash itself, as opposed to room temperature. The moromi's 品温 is logged daily and steered degree by degree; a low, slow curve is the classic ginjo approach.
- 日本酒度（にほんしゅど） — sake meter value, a specific-gravity reading taken with a 日本酒度計. Plus means less residual sugar (drier), minus means sweeter; in the moromi it climbs day by day as sugar turns into alcohol, telling the 杜氏 when to press.
- 追い水（おいみず） — water added late in the moromi to cool it down, slow fermentation, or adjust the final balance and strength.
- 四段仕込み（よだんじこみ） — a fourth addition after 留添, often steamed rice, koji, or a sweet 甘酒-style mash, to give back sweetness and body.
- 醸造アルコール（じょうぞうアルコール） — distilled alcohol added near the end of the moromi, before pressing. Up to 10% of the rice weight is allowed in 本醸造 and 吟醸; 純米 sake has none.
**例文**
留添（とめぞえ）から二週間、品温（ひんおん）を八度前後に保ちながら日本酒度を毎日測り、搾る直前に醸造アルコールを加えた。
||Two weeks after the final mash addition, we held the mash at about 8°C, took the sake meter value every day, and added distilled brewer's alcohol just before pressing.||
