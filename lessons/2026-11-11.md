**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月11日**

**漢字**
- 櫂 — 音読み: トウ / 訓読み: かい — oar, paddle; in a brewery, the long wooden paddle used to stir starters and mash. Not a 常用 kanji, and the on-reading is almost never used: in sake it's always かい. Common sake word: 櫂入れ（かいいれ）.

**ことば**
- 櫂入れ（かいいれ） — stirring the mash with a paddle. Done through 三段仕込み（さんだんじこみ） and the moromi period to even out the temperature and mix rice, kōji and yeast; ginjō brewers keep it gentle and infrequent.
- 櫂棒（かいぼう） — the paddle itself: a long pole with a flat board at the end. Many breweries now also mix by machine or with air, but the 櫂棒 is still the classic tool.
- 品温（ひんおん） — the temperature of the mash itself (not of the room). Brewers record it every day; a ginjō moromi is kept cold, around 10°C or a little above at its peak.
- 日本酒度（にほんしゅど） — sake meter value, a specific-gravity reading taken with a hydrometer, the 日本酒度計（にほんしゅどけい）. It starts far into the minus side and climbs as the sugar ferments away. On a finished label, plus leans 辛口（からくち） and minus leans 甘口（あまくち）, though acidity changes how it actually tastes.
- 醪日数（もろみにっすう） — the number of days of moromi fermentation, usually counted from 留添（とめぞえ） to pressing. Roughly three weeks for everyday sake; a month or more for cold ginjō mashes.

**例文**
醪日数が進むにつれて、蔵人（くらびと）は毎日品温と日本酒度を測（はか）り、櫂入れの回数（かいすう）を調整（ちょうせい）する。
||As the days of fermentation go by, the brewers measure the mash temperature and the sake meter value every day and adjust how often they stir with the paddle.||
