**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月15日**

**漢字**
- 貯 — 音読み: チョ / 訓読み: たくわ-える — to store, save up. After pressing and filtering, most sake rests in tanks for months before it is released; that storage is 貯蔵（ちょぞう）.
- 詰 — 音読み: キツ / 訓読み: つ-める、つ-まる — to pack, fill. In sake it means filling bottles or tanks: 瓶詰（びんづ）め, bottling, and the label word 生詰め below.

**ことば**
- 火入れ（ひいれ） — pasteurization. The sake is gently heated to about 60-65°C, which kills spoilage bacteria and stops leftover kōji enzymes. The standard practice is twice: once before storage, and once at bottling.
- 火落ち（ひおち） — spoilage caused by 火落菌（ひおちきん）, alcohol-tolerant lactic acid bacteria that make sake cloudy, sour and off-smelling. Preventing it is the original reason for 火入れ.
- 生酒（なまざけ） — sake that has never been pasteurized at all. Fresh and lively, but it must be kept cold. Also called 本生（ほんなま）.
- 生詰め（なまづめ） — pasteurized once before storage, but not again at bottling. The autumn favorite ひやおろし is this style.
- 生貯蔵酒（なまちょぞうしゅ） — the reverse: stored unpasteurized, then pasteurized once at bottling. Easy to mix up with 生詰め, so ask which half of the process the 生 refers to.

**例文**
火落ちを防（ふせ）ぐため、多くの蔵（くら）では貯蔵（ちょぞう）の前と瓶詰（びんづ）めの時に二回火入れをするが、生酒は一度（いちど）も火入れをせず、生詰めは瓶詰めの時の火入れだけを省（はぶ）く。
||To prevent spoilage, most breweries pasteurize twice, before storage and again at bottling; unpasteurized sake is never heated at all, while the "fresh-bottled" style skips only the pasteurization at bottling.||
