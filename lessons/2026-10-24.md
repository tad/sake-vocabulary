**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 10月24日**
**漢字**
- 吟 — 音読み: ギン / 訓読み: — — to chant, recite; to examine carefully. In sake it means "carefully selected, carefully made," and it is the heart of 吟醸（ぎんじょう）, the label word for meticulously brewed, highly polished sake.
- 純 — 音読み: ジュン / 訓読み: — — pure, unmixed. On a label it promises nothing but rice, kōji and water: 純米（じゅんまい）, with no added distilled alcohol.
**ことば**
- 特定名称酒（とくていめいしょうしゅ） — "special designation sake," the eight premium categories defined by the National Tax Agency's labeling standards. Each category has rules for ingredients and polishing ratio, and every one requires graded rice and a kōji-rice ratio of at least 15%. These standards replaced the old 級別制度（きゅうべつせいど）, the tax-based first-grade/second-grade system abolished in 1992.
- 普通酒（ふつうしゅ） — ordinary or table sake: everything that falls outside the special designations. This is still the majority of sake made, and much of it is honest everyday drinking, not a mark of poor quality.
- 純米酒（じゅんまいしゅ） — sake made only from rice, kōji and water. Since 2004 there has been no minimum polishing ratio, but the ratio must be printed on the label. 特別純米酒 is its "special" sibling.
- 吟醸造り（ぎんじょうづくり） — the ginjo way of brewing: highly polished rice and a slow, cold fermentation that brings out fruity, floral aromas. The ginjo categories require this method, with rice polished to 60% or less (50% or less for 大吟醸).
**例文**
この蔵（くら）では、普通酒も特定名称酒も造っているが、看板はやはり吟醸造りの純米酒だ。
||This brewery makes both ordinary table sake and premium special-designation sake, but its signature is, as you'd expect, a pure-rice sake brewed in the ginjo style.||
