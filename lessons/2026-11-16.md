**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月16日**

**漢字**
- 熟 — 音読み: ジュク / 訓読み: う-れる — ripe; to ripen, mature. Sake in storage isn't just waiting, it's ripening, and this is the kanji for it: 熟成（じゅくせい）, and on labels 長期熟成酒（ちょうきじゅくせいしゅ）, long-aged sake.
- 呑 — 音読み: ドン / 訓読み: の-む — to swallow, drink (a more colorful variant of 飲む). In the brewery it names the tap at the bottom of a storage tank, 呑口（のみくち）, and the summer tasting below.

**ことば**
- 熟成（じゅくせい） — maturation, aging. Most sake rests in tank for months after its first pasteurization so the rough edges round off. Aged for years, it becomes 長期熟成酒 or 古酒（こしゅ）, and its color deepens toward amber.
- 蛇管（じゃかん） — coiled pipe (literally "snake pipe") used for pasteurizing: sake flows through a coil sitting in hot water and comes out at about 60-65°C. Many breweries now use plate heat exchangers instead, but the word is still everyday brewery talk.
- 呑み切り（のみきり） — the summer check of stored sake: each tank's tap is opened and a sample drawn to see how it is maturing and whether anything has gone wrong (such as 火落ち（ひおち）). Traditionally a big event around June to August, often with outside technical advisors invited.
- 老香（ひねか） — a stale, heavy aged off-aroma (think pickled vegetables or burnt sugar) caused by heat, light, or too long in storage. Unpasteurized sake can develop its own version, called 生老香（なまひね）.

**例文**
蛇管で火入れ（ひいれ）して貯蔵（ちょぞう）した酒を、毎年夏の呑み切りで杜氏（とうじ）がタンクごとに調べ、熟成の具合（ぐあい）と老香が出ていないかを確（たし）かめる。
||Every summer, at the tank-tap tasting, the master brewer goes tank by tank through the sake that was pasteurized in the coiled pipe and put into storage, checking how its maturation is coming along and making sure no stale, aged off-aroma has appeared.||
