**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 10月26日**
**漢字**
- 清 — 音読み: セイ、ショウ / 訓読み: きよ-い、きよ-まる、きよ-める — clear, pure, clean. It's the first character of 清酒, the legal word for sake, and the reason "clear" sits at the heart of how the law defines it.
- 増 — 音読み: ゾウ / 訓読み: ま-す、ふ-える、ふ-やす — to increase, add to. In sake it mostly appears in the history of stretching volume: 増醸（ぞうじょう, brewing up the yield with additives）and 三増酒.

**ことば**
- 清酒（せいしゅ） — the legal category name for sake under the Liquor Tax Act: made from rice, kōji and water (plus permitted adjuncts), fermented and then strained (こしたもの), under 22% alcohol. Because it must be strained, unpressed どぶろく is not 清酒. Labels and official statistics use this word; 日本酒 is the everyday one.
- 酒税法（しゅぜいほう） — the Liquor Tax Act, which defines the liquor categories (清酒 included), sets tax rates, and licenses brewers. The labeling standard you met yesterday is a notice issued under this law.
- 三増酒（さんぞうしゅ） — short for 三倍増醸清酒（さんばいぞうじょうせいしゅ）: "triple-yield sake," a postwar rice-shortage practice of adding distilled alcohol, sugars (糖類) and acidulants (酸味料) to roughly triple the volume from the same rice. The 2006 revision of the Liquor Tax Act capped adjuncts at half the rice weight, so it can no longer be sold as 清酒. It's a large part of why 普通酒 earned a bad reputation.
- 合成清酒（ごうせいせいしゅ） — "synthetic sake": a sake-like drink built mainly from alcohol, sugars and amino acids rather than fermented rice, developed around 1920 amid rice shortages. Still a separate legal category; today it's mostly cheap cooking sake.

**例文**
戦後の米不足の時代には三増酒が広く造られたが、二〇〇六年の酒税法改正によって、もう清酒とは名乗れなくなった。
||In the postwar years of rice shortage, triple-yield sake stretched with added alcohol and sugar was made widely, but after the 2006 revision of the Liquor Tax Act it could no longer be sold under the legal name for sake.||
