**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月18日**

**漢字**
- 吟 — 音読み: ギン / 訓読み: （常用の訓読みなし） — to chant, recite; to examine closely (as in 吟味（ぎんみ）, careful scrutiny). In sake it stands for meticulous selection and craft, and it's the heart of 吟醸（ぎんじょう）, the label word for carefully brewed, highly polished sake.
- 純 — 音読み: ジュン / 訓読み: （常用の訓読みなし） — pure, unmixed. On a label it promises nothing but rice, kōji and water, with no added distilled alcohol: 純米（じゅんまい）.

**ことば**
- 特定名称酒（とくていめいしょうしゅ） — "special designation sake": the eight premium categories set by the National Tax Agency's labeling standards (純米, 吟醸 and 本醸造 families). All of them require rice graded 3等 or better and a kōji-rice share of at least 15%. They replaced the old tax-based 級別制度（きゅうべつせいど）, the first-grade/second-grade system, fully abolished in 1992.
- 普通酒（ふつうしゅ） — ordinary or table sake: everything outside the special designations. It is still the majority of sake made, and much of it is honest everyday drinking, not a mark of poor quality.
- 純米酒（じゅんまいしゅ） — sake made only from rice, kōji and water. Since 2004 there is no minimum polishing ratio, but the 精米歩合（せいまいぶあい） must be printed on the label.
- 吟醸造り（ぎんじょうづくり） — the ginjo way of brewing: well-polished rice and a slow, cold fermentation that brings out fruity, floral aromas. The ginjo grades require it, with rice polished to 60% or less (50% or less for 大吟醸（だいぎんじょう）).

**例文**
特定名称酒の中でも、この蔵（くら）の看板（かんばん）は吟醸造りの純米酒だが、地元（じもと）で一番売（う）れているのは普通酒だ。
||Of all its premium special-designation sakes, this brewery's signature is a pure-rice sake brewed the ginjo way, but what sells best locally is its ordinary table sake.||
