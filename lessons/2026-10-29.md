**漢字**
- 精 — 音読み: セイ、ショウ / 訓読み: （常用の訓読みなし） — refined, polished; essence. Sake begins with polishing the rice, 精米（せいまい）, and the whole grade system rests on how far the grain is milled.
- 磨 — 音読み: マ / 訓読み: みが-く — to polish, to grind. Brewers say the rice is 磨（みが）かれた, "polished down," and the word even appears in product names, e.g. 獺祭（だっさい） 磨き二割三分（みがきにわりさんぶ）.

**ことば**
- 酒造好適米（しゅぞうこうてきまい） — rice suited to sake brewing; "sake rice." The formal term for varieties like 山田錦（やまだにしき） and 五百万石（ごひゃくまんごく）: large grains, low protein, and usually a clear starchy core. In everyday talk it's shortened to 酒米（さかまい）.
- 精米歩合（せいまいぶあい） — polishing ratio. It's the percentage of the grain that *remains*, not what's removed: 60% means 40% was milled away. It's printed on labels and is one of the bases of the premium grades.
- 心白（しんぱく） — the opaque white core at the center of a sake-rice grain. Its starch is loosely packed, so kōji mold can grow into it easily; a large, centered 心白 is prized.
- 精米機（せいまいき） — rice-polishing machine. For sake this usually means a vertical mill, 縦型精米機（たてがたせいまいき）, which grinds slowly so the grains don't overheat or crack.

**例文**
心白（しんぱく）の大きな酒米（さかまい）を精米機でゆっくり磨き、精米歩合五十パーセントまで仕上げた。
||We slowly polished sake rice with a large white core in the polishing machine, bringing it down to a 50% polishing ratio.||
