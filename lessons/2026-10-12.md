**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 10月12日**
**漢字**
- 酛 — 音読み: — / 訓読み: もと — yeast starter. A 国字（こくじ）, a character made in Japan, with no on-reading: 酉 (the wine jar) plus 元 (origin). You'll meet it in every starter method's name: 生酛, 山廃酛, 速醸酛.
- 酵 — 音読み: コウ / 訓読み: — — fermentation, leaven. The heart of 発酵（はっこう）, fermentation, and of 酵母, the yeast that turns sugar into alcohol.
**ことば**
- 酒母（しゅぼ） — the yeast starter: a small, dense batch of koji, steamed rice and water in which a large, healthy yeast population is grown before the main mash. 酒母 is the formal term; 酛 is the older brewer's word for the same thing.
- 酵母（こうぼ） — yeast. Chosen strains (often the numbered 協会酵母 distributed by the Brewing Society of Japan) shape a sake's aroma as much as almost any other choice in the kura.
- 乳酸（にゅうさん） — lactic acid. The starter must be acidic to keep wild yeasts and bacteria out while the sake yeast multiplies; how that acid gets there is what separates the starter methods.
- 速醸酛（そくじょうもと） — the quick starter method: commercially made lactic acid is added at the outset instead of letting lactic bacteria produce it. Developed at the national brewing research institute around 1910, it takes about two weeks instead of roughly four, and it is used for the large majority of sake made today.
**例文**
速醸酛では、仕込み水（しこみみず）に乳酸を加えてから麹と蒸米を入れ、酵母を健全に増やして酒母を育てる。
||In the quick starter method, lactic acid is added to the brewing water first, then the kōji and steamed rice go in, and the yeast is left to multiply healthily, raising the yeast starter.||
