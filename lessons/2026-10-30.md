**漢字**
- 糠 — 音読み: コウ / 訓読み: ぬか — rice bran. Everything the polishing machine grinds away becomes 糠. The brown outer layers are 赤糠（あかぬか）, used for animal feed and pickling; the paler inner layers are 白糠（しろぬか）, sold as rice flour for 煎餅（せんべい） and sweets. Its fats and proteins are exactly what brewers want gone from the grain.
- 浸 — 音読み: シン / 訓読み: ひた-す、ひた-る — to soak, to steep. It's the kanji behind the soaking step before steaming, 浸漬（しんせき）.

**ことば**
- 枯（か）らし — the rest after polishing. Milling heats and dries the grain, so the rice is left for a few weeks to cool and take moisture back from the air. Skip it and the grains crack when they hit water.
- 洗米（せんまい） — washing the polished rice to strip off the leftover 糠 powder. For 吟醸（ぎんじょう） rice it's done quickly in small batches, because the grain starts drinking water the moment it's wet.
- 浸漬（しんせき） — soaking the washed rice before steaming. Breweries say しんせき; dictionaries also give しんし, and you'll hear both.
- 限定吸水（げんていきゅうすい） — timed, limited soaking. Highly polished rice absorbs water fast, so it's soaked by stopwatch, sometimes for only seconds or minutes, and weighed to hit a target uptake (often around 30%). A hallmark of careful 吟醸 work.

**例文**
精米（せいまい）を終（お）えた米は二週間（にしゅうかん）ほど枯（か）らしてから洗米（せんまい）し、ストップウォッチを片手（かたて）に限定吸水（げんていきゅうすい）で浸漬（しんせき）する。
||Once polished, the rice rests for about two weeks. Then it's washed and soaked with limited water absorption, stopwatch in hand.||
