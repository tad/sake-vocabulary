**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 10月10日**
**漢字**
- 床 — 音読み: ショウ / 訓読み: とこ、ゆか — bed, floor. In the 麹室 it's the long wooden work table where the rice is spread, spored, and kneaded. You'll see it in 床もみ and 麹床（こうじどこ）.
- 盛 — 音読み: セイ、ジョウ / 訓読み: も-る、さか-る、さか-ん — to heap up or pile, as when serving food; also "to flourish." In the kōji room, 盛り is the step where the rice gets heaped into smaller containers.
**ことば**
- 床もみ（とこもみ） — rubbing the spores into the rice on the table right after 種切り. It's done by hand so every grain is coated evenly. The rice is then piled and wrapped in cloth to hold its warmth. Also written 床揉み.
- 切り返し（きりかえし） — roughly half a day later, the rice has warmed and clumped together. The team unwraps it, breaks up the clumps, evens out temperature and moisture, and wraps it again.
- 盛り（もり） — once the mold is growing hard and producing its own heat, the rice is split into smaller portions (trays, boxes, or a bed) so the brewers can control heat and drying. 仲仕事（なかしごと） and 仕舞仕事（しまいしごと） follow from here.
- 麹蓋（こうじぶた） — the small, shallow cedar tray used in the most traditional method, 麹蓋法（こうじぶたほう）. It's labor-intensive, so many kura use larger 箱（はこ） boxes or machines instead. When a label or tech sheet mentions 蓋麹（ふたこうじ）, it signals that hands-on approach.
**例文**
切り返しを終えた麹は、翌日（よくじつ）の盛りで麹蓋に小分けされ、温度を細かく管理される。
||Once the clumps have been broken up and rewrapped, the kōji is divided into small cedar trays during the next day's portioning step, where its temperature is carefully controlled.||
