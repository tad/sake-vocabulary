**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月7日**
**漢字**
- 卸 — 音読み: シャ（常用外） / 訓読み: おろ-す — to unload, to grind down; also "to sell wholesale". In sake it's the 卸 of 山卸し（やまおろし）, the hard grinding work of the traditional starter. Outside the brewery you'll meet it in 卸売（おろしうり）, wholesale.
- 廃 — 音読み: ハイ / 訓読み: すた-れる、すた-る — to abolish, to fall out of use. It's the 廃 in 山廃（やまはい）, short for 山卸廃止酛（やまおろしはいしもと）: "the starter where yamaoroshi was abolished".

**ことば**
- 生酛（きもと） — the traditional Edo-period starter method. No acid is added: lactic acid bacteria from the brewery's own surroundings make the acid naturally, and only then does the yeast take over. It takes about four weeks and gives sake that is often richer and more savory. Note the unusual reading of 生 as き, as in 生一本（きいっぽん）.
- 山卸し（やまおろし） — grinding the rice and kōji of a kimoto starter into a paste with wooden poles, done by brewers in shifts on cold nights. Also called 酛摺り（もとすり）.
- 半切（はんぎり） — the wide, shallow wooden tubs where kimoto starters are made and ground; also written 半切り桶（はんぎりおけ）. Shallow so the starter stays cold and is easy to work.
- 山廃酛（やまはいもと） — the kimoto variant without yamaoroshi. Around 1909, researchers found that if the water and kōji are mixed first, the kōji's enzymes dissolve the rice on their own, so the grinding wasn't needed. On labels you'll usually see just 山廃（やまはい）.

**例文**
山卸し（やまおろし）の手間（てま）を省（はぶ）いた山廃酛（やまはいもと）も、生酛（きもと）と同じく、蔵（くら）にいる自然（しぜん）の乳酸菌（にゅうさんきん）の力（ちから）で酒母（しゅぼ）を育てる。
||Yamahai, which skips the hard work of yamaoroshi, raises its yeast starter just like kimoto does, through the power of the natural lactic acid bacteria living in the brewery.||
