**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月21日**

**漢字**
- 特 — 音読み: トク / 訓読み: (常用の訓読みなし) — special, particular. On sake labels it marks the "special" step up within a grade: 特別純米酒（とくべつじゅんまいしゅ）. Before 1992 it also topped the old tax grades as 特級（とっきゅう）; note how トク becomes とっ before き.
- 級 — 音読み: キュウ / 訓読み: (常用の訓読みなし) — rank, class, grade. The key kanji of the old tax-based grading system, 級別制度（きゅうべつせいど）, with its 特級・一級（いっきゅう）・二級（にきゅう） sake.

**ことば**
- 特別純米酒（とくべつじゅんまいしゅ） — tokubetsu junmai: a junmai sake (no added alcohol) with rice polished to 60% or less, OR made by a "special brewing method." If the claim is the special method, the label has to explain what it is.
- 特別本醸造酒（とくべつほんじょうぞうしゅ） — tokubetsu honjozo: the same idea for honjozo (a small amount of brewing alcohol added): 60% or less polishing, or a special method explained on the label. With these two, all eight 特定名称酒 categories are complete.
- 級別制度（きゅうべつせいど） — the old grading system: sake was taxed by grade (特級, 一級, 二級), and the grade was decided by official inspection, not by how it was brewed. Phased out from 1989 and fully abolished in 1992, when the 特定名称酒 categories took over.
- 特級酒（とっきゅうしゅ） — "special-grade" sake under the old system: the top grade and the most heavily taxed. You still meet the word in older Japanese novels, films and advertisements.
- 二級酒（にきゅうしゅ） — "second-grade" sake, the lowest and cheapest grade. Some proud breweries skipped the inspection on purpose and sold excellent sake as 二級酒, so a cheap 二級 was not always a lesser sake.

**例文**
昔は特級酒や二級酒という級別制度があったが、今の特別純米酒の「特別」は税金（ぜいきん）の区別（くべつ）ではなく、製法（せいほう）の違（ちが）いを表（あらわ）している。
||In the old days there was a grading system with special-grade and second-grade sake, but the "special" in today's tokubetsu junmai doesn't mark a tax category: it shows a difference in how the sake was made.||
