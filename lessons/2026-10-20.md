**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 10月20日**
**漢字**
- 炭 — 音読み: タン / 訓読み: すみ — charcoal; carbon. Powdered charcoal is the classic tool for stripping color and off-flavors from pressed sake, so it sits behind the whole 無濾過 debate (活性炭, 炭素濾過).
- 斗 — 音読み: ト / 訓読み: — — the old unit of volume, one 斗 ≈ 18 liters (ten 升). It survives in the brewery in vessel names like 斗瓶 and in the 一斗瓶 and 四斗樽 still used for festive casks.
**ことば**
- 槽口（ふなくち） — the spout of the press, where the sake first runs out. On labels it signals sake bottled straight from the press, usually unpasteurized and undiluted.
- 斗瓶囲い（とびんがこい） — collecting choice sake, often drip-pressed 雫, in 18-liter glass jugs and storing each jug separately. The brewer tastes jug by jug and picks the best, classically for competition entries; also 斗瓶取り（とびんどり）.
- 活性炭（かっせいたん） — activated charcoal, mixed into the sake and then filtered out to adjust color and flavor. Used lightly it polishes; used heavily it strips character, which is why many kura now advertise using little or none.
- 遠心分離（えんしんぶんり） — centrifugal separation: spinning the moromi to separate sake from lees instead of pressing. Expensive and rare, valued for a clean, aromatic result with no pressure applied.
- 濁り酒（にごりざけ） — cloudy sake, pressed through a coarse mesh so fine lees stay in. Legally it is still filtered sake, unlike unpressed どぶろく; usually written にごり酒 on labels.
**例文**
槽口（ふなくち）から滴る酒を斗瓶囲い（とびんがこい）で分け、活性炭（かっせいたん）は使わずに出荷した。
||The sake trickling from the press spout was collected separately in 18-liter glass jugs and shipped without any activated charcoal.||
