**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月26日**

**漢字**
- 斗 — 音読み: ト / 訓読み: （常用の訓読みなし） — a traditional unit of volume, about 18 liters (10 升（しょう）). Brewers still think in 斗 and 升, as in 斗瓶（とびん）.
- 瓶 — 音読み: ビン、ヘイ / 訓読み: かめ — bottle; (as かめ) an earthenware jar. Every sake label and store shelf uses it: 一升瓶（いっしょうびん）, and you already met it in 瓶詰（びんづ）め.

**ことば**
- 斗瓶（とびん） — a large glass bottle holding one 斗 (about 18 liters). Used in the brewery to collect and rest small, special batches of sake.
- 斗瓶囲い（とびんがこい） — sake whose best drops (often from 袋吊（ふくろづ）り) are collected into 斗瓶 and kept apart. Traditionally the brewery's entry for 品評会（ひんぴょうかい） competitions; on a label it signals a premium bottling.
- 槽口（ふなくち） — the spout of the 槽（ふね） where freshly pressed sake flows out. Labels saying ふなくち mean sake bottled straight from the press, usually unpasteurized and undiluted.
- 一升瓶（いっしょうびん） — the classic 1.8-liter sake bottle (one 升). The 四合瓶（よんごうびん） (720 ml) is its smaller everyday partner.

**例文**
蔵（くら）では、袋（ふくろ）から落（お）ちる雫（しずく）を斗瓶に集（あつ）めて斗瓶囲いにし、残りの酒は槽口から受けて一升瓶に詰（つ）めた。
||At the brewery, the drops falling from the bags were gathered into large glass bottles and kept apart as a premium batch, and the rest of the sake was caught at the press spout and bottled in big 1.8-liter bottles.||
