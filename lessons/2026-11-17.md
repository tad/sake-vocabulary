**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月17日**

**漢字**
- 割 — 音読み: カツ / 訓読み: わ-る、わり、さ-く — to split, divide; to dilute. In the brewery it means cutting sake with water before bottling, 割水（わりみず）, the same 割 as the 水割（みずわ）り on a whisky menu. (You also met it in polishing ratios like 二割三分.)
- 調 — 音読み: チョウ / 訓読み: しら-べる、ととの-える — to investigate; to adjust, put in order. In sake it means bringing several tanks together into one consistent finished product: 調合（ちょうごう）.

**ことば**
- 調合（ちょうごう） — blending: combining sake from different tanks before bottling. It's how a brewery keeps a flagship label tasting the same from year to year, a quiet but important skill of the 杜氏（とうじ）.
- 割水（わりみず） — adding water to bring sake down from press strength (usually about 17-20% alcohol) to the target, often about 15-16%. Done after storage, just before bottling; the water's quality matters as much here as in brewing.
- 原酒（げんしゅ） — undiluted sake: no water added after pressing (the labeling rules allow only a tiny adjustment, under 1% alcohol). Heavier and more powerful in the glass; often good over ice.
- 瓶燗火入れ（びんかんひいれ） — pasteurizing sake after it's bottled, by warming the bottles in hot water and then cooling them quickly. Slower and more work than the coiled-pipe method, but it keeps aroma and freshness, so many small breweries use it for premium bottlings.
- 秋上がり（あきあがり） — sake that has matured well over the summer and comes into its own in autumn, rounder and more harmonious. Its opposite, sake that has faded or gone flat over the summer, is 秋落（あきお）ち. Autumn releases such as ひやおろし are judged by exactly this.

**例文**
夏を越（こ）して秋上がりした酒を調合し、割水をせずに原酒のまま瓶燗火入れで仕上（しあ）げた。
||We blended sake that had matured beautifully over the summer, added no water, and finished it at full strength, pasteurizing it in the bottle.||
