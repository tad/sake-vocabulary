**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 11月22日**

**漢字**
- 清 — 音読み: セイ、ショウ / 訓読み: きよ-い、きよ-まる、きよ-める — clear, pure, clean. It is the legal word for sake itself: 清酒（せいしゅ）, "clear sake," the name printed on every bottle's label.
- 税 — 音読み: ゼイ / 訓読み: (常用の訓読みなし) — tax. Sake has always been tied to tax: 酒税（しゅぜい）, the liquor tax, decides what may legally be called sake.

**ことば**
- 清酒（せいしゅ） — the legal and label term for sake. Under the liquor tax law it must be made from rice, rice kōji and water (plus permitted extras like brewing alcohol), fermented, and then strained, with less than 22% alcohol. The word 日本酒 is what people say; 清酒 is what the label says.
- 酒税法（しゅぜいほう） — the Liquor Tax Act. It defines every category of alcoholic drink in Japan, including 清酒 and the 特定名称酒 grades, and brewers need a license under it to make sake at all.
- どぶろく（濁酒） — unstrained sake: the mash is drunk as is, rice solids and all. Because it is never strained, it is legally NOT 清酒 but "other brewed alcohol." Usually written in kana.
- 合成清酒（ごうせいせいしゅ） — "synthetic sake": a drink made by blending alcohol with sugars, acids and flavorings to imitate sake, with little or no rice fermentation. A separate legal category, today mostly used for cooking.
- 三増酒（さんぞうしゅ） — short for 三倍増醸酒（さんばいぞうじょうしゅ）: a postwar method from the rice shortage, adding diluted alcohol, sugar and acids to the mash to roughly triple the yield. After the 2006 revision of the law it can no longer be sold as 清酒.

**例文**
酒税法では、醪（もろみ）をこさないどぶろくは清酒と呼べないが、目の粗（あら）い布（ぬの）でこしたにごり酒は清酒になる。
||Under the Liquor Tax Act, doburoku, whose mash is never strained, can't be called seishu, but nigori sake, strained through a coarse-weave cloth, counts as seishu.||
