**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 10月23日**
**漢字**
- 割 — 音読み: カツ / 訓読み: わ-る、わり、さ-く — to split, divide; to dilute. In the kura it means cutting sake with water before bottling (割水), and it's the same 割 you'll see on the 水割り of a highball menu.
- 調 — 音読み: チョウ / 訓読み: しら-べる、ととの-える — to investigate; to adjust, put in order. In sake it means bringing tanks together into a finished, consistent product (調合).
**ことば**
- 調合（ちょうごう） — blending: combining sake from different tanks before bottling. It's how a kura keeps a flagship label consistent from year to year, and it's a quiet, under-discussed skill of the 杜氏（とうじ）.
- 割水（わりみず） — adding water to bring sake down from press strength (usually around 17–20%) to the target alcohol, often around 15–16%. Done after storage, before bottling; the water's quality matters as much as for brewing.
- 原酒（げんしゅ） — undiluted sake: no water added after pressing (labeling rules allow only a tiny adjustment, under 1% alcohol). Expect more weight, power and alcohol in the glass; it's often good over ice.
- 瓶燗火入れ（びんかんひいれ） — pasteurizing sake after it's in the bottle by warming the bottles in hot water, then cooling quickly. Slower and more labor-intensive than the 蛇管 method, but it traps aroma and freshness; many small kura use it for premium bottlings.
- 秋上がり（あきあがり） — sake that has matured well over the summer and comes into its own by autumn, rounder and more harmonious. Its opposite, sake that has faded or gone flat over summer, is 秋落ち（あきおち）.
**例文**
夏を越して秋上がりした酒を調合（ちょうごう）し、割水をせずに原酒のまま瓶燗（びんかん）火入れで仕上げた。
||We blended sake that had matured beautifully over the summer, added no water, and finished it at full strength, pasteurizing it in the bottle.||
