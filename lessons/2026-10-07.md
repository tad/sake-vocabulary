**🍶 今日の酒ことば — 10月7日**
**漢字**
- 蒸 — 音読み: ジョウ / 訓読み: む-す、む-れる、む-らす — to steam. Sake rice is steamed, not boiled. Steaming softens the starch so kōji and enzymes can work on it, while each grain stays separate and fairly dry. Common sake word: 蒸米（むしまい）.
- 甑 — 音読み: ソウ / 訓読み: こしき — the traditional rice-steaming vessel, set over a big iron cauldron called a 和釜（わがま）. Outside the kura you rarely see it, but it lives on in the word 甑倒し（こしきだおし）.
**ことば**
- 蒸米（むしまい） — steamed rice for brewing. All of the rice used in brewing starts out as 蒸米, whether it goes to the kōji, the starter, or the additions to the mash. Each morning the brewers check it by hand.
- 外硬内軟（がいこうないなん） — "firm outside, soft inside," the classic goal for steamed rice. A dry, firm surface lets kōji mold grow into the grain instead of leaving it sticky.
- 放冷（ほうれい） — cooling the steamed rice to its target temperature, either spread out on cloths or run through a 放冷機（ほうれいき）. Rice headed for the kōji room is kept warmer than rice going straight into the tank.
- 甑倒し（こしきだおし） — literally "toppling the koshiki." It's the last steaming of the season, after which the steamer isn't needed. Kura usually celebrate with a small gathering, since it means the hardest part of the brewing season is over.
**例文**
外硬内軟に仕上がった蒸米を放冷し、麹室（こうじむろ）へ運ぶ。今季もようやく甑倒しを迎えた。
||The steamed rice comes out firm outside and soft inside, gets cooled, and is carried to the kōji room. This season, too, we've finally reached the last steaming.||
